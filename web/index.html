The cleanup pass

Speech is messy. Nobody wants a transcript.

Every other dictation tool hands you exactly what you said, false starts and all — so you spend the time you saved fixing commas. Wisp runs the raw transcript through Apple's on-device model before a single character reaches your cursor.

Filler words go. Punctuation and capitalisation appear. "new paragraph" becomes a new paragraph instead of the literal words. Your phrasing and your meaning stay exactly as you said them — it's a typist, not a ghostwriter.

Editing by voice

Select something first, and Wisp rewrites instead.

There's no command word to remember and no mode to toggle. Wisp checks whether the app you're in has text selected the moment you press the key.

Nothing selected, and your words are dictated. Something selected, and your words are treated as an instruction for that text — "make this a bullet list", "cut it down to two sentences", "say this more warmly" — and the selection is replaced with the result.

One gesture, start to finish

How it works

  1. Hold the key

    Right ⌥ by default, or Right ⌘ or fn. Wisp notes which app you're in and whether you've selected anything.

  2. Talk

    Words appear in a small floating pill as they're recognised, so you can see it's hearing you. Recognition runs while you speak, not after.

  3. Let go

    The cleanup pass runs and the finished text is pasted at your cursor. Your clipboard is put back exactly as you left it.

Architecture

Nothing leaves the machine.

This isn't a privacy policy promising deletion — there's no server to delete anything from. Wisp has no account, no telemetry and no network calls after the first model download.

Install

Build it and run it.

# first build downloads the Parakeet models (~600 MB)
git clone …/Wisp && cd Wisp
./Scripts/build.sh
open build/Wisp.app

Wisp lives in the menu bar with no Dock icon. macOS will ask for Microphone access, and for Accessibility — it needs that to see your hotkey and to paste into other apps.

  • macOS 26 or later, Apple silicon
  • Apple Intelligence enabled for the cleanup pass (dictation still works without it)
  • Roughly 600 MB on disk for the speech models
